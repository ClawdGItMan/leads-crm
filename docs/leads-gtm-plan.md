# Leads GTM Plan

## What `Leads` Is

`Leads` is a lean CRM for small companies that want one place for leads, contacts, summaries, and a simple dashboard without the overhead of an all-in-one platform. The wedge is speed: fast setup, clean UX, no crypto baggage, and no enterprise bloat.

## 1) Best Target Customer Profiles

### Primary ICPs

- Founder-led B2B service firms with 2-15 people.
- Agencies and consultancies that live in email, referrals, and follow-up.
- Small sales teams that need a shared CRM but do not want HubSpot complexity.
- Solo operators and fractional sellers who want a clean lead tracker plus summaries.

### Best-fit use cases

- Turning inbound leads into a visible pipeline.
- Keeping company and contact data organized after handoffs from spreadsheets.
- Reviewing recent activity and next steps without manually writing summaries.
- Replacing a messy mix of Notion, Sheets, and inbox follow-up with one lightweight CRM.

### Not the first target

- Large enterprises with deep workflow requirements.
- Teams that need full marketing automation, help desk, or advanced revenue ops on day one.
- Sales orgs that depend on heavy dialer, SMS, and sequencing infrastructure.

## 2) Positioning and Messaging Pillars

### Positioning statement

`Leads` is the simple CRM for small teams that want to track leads, manage contacts, and get clear summaries without adopting a giant platform.

### Messaging pillars

- Simple enough to adopt in a day, not a month.
- Clean enough to stay open all day.
- Flexible enough to fit many company types.
- Helpful enough to reduce manual follow-up and status reporting.

### Proof points to emphasize

- Dashboard, leads, contacts, and summaries are the core product, not add-ons.
- Blank-slate setup means teams can shape it to their own process.
- AI summaries reduce admin work and create a recurring reason to return.
- Shared CRM structure supports team use without forcing enterprise complexity.

## 3) Acquisition Channels

### Highest-priority channels

- Founder-led outbound to agencies, consultancies, and B2B service firms.
- SEO around practical intent keywords like `simple CRM`, `CRM for agencies`, `lead tracker`, `contact manager`, and `CRM alternative`.
- Product-led demo loops with a public homepage, live demo, and fast trial or sandbox.
- Marketplaces and integration directories once the first connectors are ready.

### Secondary channels

- LinkedIn content from the founder showing before/after workflows and CRM cleanup.
- Short-form videos showing how fast a lead can move from capture to summary.
- Partner/referral channel with fractional sales consultants, agency operators, and CRM implementers.
- Comparison pages targeting alternatives to HubSpot, Pipedrive, Close, Attio, and folk.

### Channel order

1. Founder-led outbound.
2. SEO and comparison pages.
3. Demo-led product marketing.
4. Partnerships and marketplaces.

## 4) Launch Campaign Ideas

### Campaign 1: `From spreadsheet to CRM in one afternoon`

- Hook: teams do not need a giant implementation to get organized.
- Asset: 2-minute demo video, landing page, and checklist.
- CTA: start a workspace and import leads in minutes.

### Campaign 2: `The clean CRM for small teams`

- Hook: a simpler alternative to bloated CRMs.
- Asset: homepage, pricing page, and side-by-side comparison.
- CTA: see the workflow, not a long feature tour.

### Campaign 3: `Weekly summary for busy teams`

- Hook: users do not just need storage, they need clarity.
- Asset: email teaser, sample summary output, and dashboard screenshots.
- CTA: generate the first summary.

### Campaign 4: `Replace Sheets + Notes + Inbox`

- Hook: many teams are not switching from a CRM, they are switching from chaos.
- Asset: migration checklist and template import guide.
- CTA: import a CSV and get started.

## 5) Payment Tiers

### Recommended launch structure

- Free Trial: 14 days, no credit card, full access.
- Starter: $19 per seat/month billed monthly, or $15 per seat/month billed annually.
- Team: $39 per seat/month billed monthly, or $29 per seat/month billed annually.
- Scale: $79 per seat/month billed monthly, or $59 per seat/month billed annually.
- Enterprise: custom pricing for security, rollout help, and advanced governance.

### Recommended packaging

- Starter: dashboard, leads, contacts, summaries, CSV import, one shared workspace, basic filters, basic activity history, and a modest monthly AI pool.
- Team: everything in Starter plus saved views, collaboration features, richer activity history, team roles, more record capacity, and a larger shared AI pool.
- Scale: everything in Team plus advanced permissions, admin controls, exports, API and integration readiness, priority support, and the largest self-serve AI pool.
- Enterprise: everything in Scale plus SSO, custom security review, migration help, onboarding, negotiated limits, and dedicated support.

### AI and usage model

- Keep the core CRM flow inside every paid plan.
- Package AI as a workspace pool, not as individual seat limits.
- Call them `AI actions` instead of credits or tokens.
- Include enough AI actions to make summaries feel core, then sell top-ups only when usage becomes meaningful.
- Suggested top-ups: 500 AI actions for $25, or 2,000 AI actions for $79.

### Why this pricing should work

- It stays above spreadsheet and DIY-tool pricing, which protects positioning.
- It stays below the cost and complexity jump buyers associate with larger CRM suites.
- It creates a clean upgrade ladder based on collaboration, governance, and usage instead of random feature gating.
- It keeps the pricing page easy to understand in under a minute.

## 6) Go-to-Market Motion

### Core motion

- Sell `Leads` as the calm operating system for small teams that live on leads, contacts, and follow-up.
- Lead with a founder-assisted, white-glove launch instead of trying to win on a mass-market self-serve funnel immediately.
- Use the product itself as the proof: import a CSV, see a clean dashboard, generate a summary, and show a working pipeline in the first session.

### Recommended funnel

1. Attract buyers through comparison pages, practical SEO, and founder-led outbound.
2. Convert interest with a simple product demo and a 14-day free trial.
3. Activate new workspaces through CSV import and a short guided setup.
4. Prove value with the first summary, recent activity feed, and visible pipeline movement.
5. Close on paid plans when teams need more seats, more collaboration, more AI, or more control.

### Best initial offer

- `We will import your current spreadsheet and set up your workspace for you.`
- This is the right launch offer because the first customer is not buying a platform strategy. They are buying speed, clarity, and relief from chaos.

## 7) 30/60/90 Day Marketing Plan

### First 30 days

- Finish homepage, pricing page, and one strong product demo page.
- Publish comparison pages for `Leads vs HubSpot`, `Leads vs Pipedrive`, and `Leads vs Close`.
- Ship the first email nurture sequence for trial users.

### Days 61-90

## 8) Risks and Assumptions

### Risks

- The market already has many CRMs, so generic messaging will not convert.
- If onboarding is not frictionless, users will default back to spreadsheets.
- A simple CRM can be mistaken for a toy if the design and summaries are not credible.
- Competing on price alone would be a race to the bottom.

### Assumptions

- The product stays lean and does not try to become a full marketing automation suite.
- The company will lead with a clean, general-purpose brand, not a niche vertical brand.
- AI summaries will remain part of the value story because they create a visible productivity benefit.
- The first customer segment is small, service-oriented teams that value speed over deep customization.

## 9) Competitive Snapshot

- HubSpot positions itself as an all-in-one platform with a free CRM, lead generation, pipeline building, and AI across marketing, sales, service, and content.
- Pipedrive emphasizes sales pipeline management and an easy sales CRM for small businesses.
- Close emphasizes speed for sales teams with built-in communication tooling and simple sales workflows.
- Attio positions around flexible, AI-native CRM structure and workspace-level adaptability.
- folk emphasizes collaborative CRM with multi-channel data capture and follow-up assistance.

### Where `Leads` should win

- Faster time-to-value than broad suites.
- Cleaner daily workflow than heavy outbound tools.
- Less configuration burden than flexible AI-native CRMs.
- Stronger habit loop through dashboard plus summaries plus follow-up.

### Where `Leads` should not try to compete first

- Full outbound sales engagement with dialing and SMS.
- Massive workflow automation breadth.
- Deep custom object modeling.
- Enterprise platform breadth across marketing, service, and support.
